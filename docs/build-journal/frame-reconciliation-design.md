# Frame reconciliation after a library change

Status: partly implemented, not enabled. Al approved preserving reachable retained photos and clearing the old cache, accepting that unknown older retained variants may need to reload online. See "Implementation status" at the end.

## Goal

Keep the selected private library available offline. After reconnect, remove media deleted from that library from playback and private caches, and add new media without replacing the current slide. Permanent deletion remains a phone action. A failed or incomplete request must never be treated as an empty library.

## Existing behavior

`KrillPrivateFeed` fetches paginated photos and videos. When supported, a library revision fences pagination and `KrillCachedMetadataRefresh` checks the revision again after fetching. Cached and in-memory playlists append new selected-space media. They do not yet remove entries missing from a complete snapshot. `KrillFrameCache` already fences private work and supports crash-recoverable cleanup for a confirmed frame deletion; its current cleanup ends the active screen and rotates the session cache namespace.

## Proposed invariants

- Reconciliation applies only to the current authenticated namespace, origin and space. A revoked lease, changed session, failed page, changing revision or disabled feed makes absence unproven.
- A missing image may be considered removed only after a complete, revision-consistent image snapshot. Apply the same rule independently to videos, and only when video enumeration is available. No revision means no destructive inference from absence.
- Before any removed item can be displayed again, invalidate active playback and outstanding private requests. Persist a recovery marker before clearing cached private bytes and playlist rows; finish cleanup after process restart if interrupted. Do not hold a lease while waiting for that same namespace to drain.
- Keep the user's authenticated library and non-private preferences. Rebuild the selected library after cleanup. Preserve unrelated sources and saved rotation choices where feasible; never silently delete media on the server or the original Google Photos item.
- A successful snapshot may append selected-space items. Preserve the current slide and do not claim a five-minute first-display guarantee merely because an item was appended to the end of a long playlist.

## Recovery protocol

- The provider must return separate photo and video results with a completeness flag and the shared revision observed during every page and after both feeds. A disabled video feed is unavailable, not an empty complete feed. Failed, unversioned or mixed-revision snapshots cannot authorize removal. An empty, complete versioned feed can.
- Compare only items belonging to the selected origin and space. At verified removal, clear the current slide and stop playback regardless of whether it contains the removed item. Stop preloads and invalidate image and video requests before cache cleanup. This bounded interruption avoids keeping private bitmaps or video surfaces alive across a namespace revocation. Restart playback from a freshly loaded, authorized playlist after cleanup; if no items remain, show the existing empty state. The preserve-current-slide rule applies to append-only updates, not removal cleanup.
- Persist a cleanup journal containing the original namespace, a new operation generation and an opaque operation identifier before invalidating playback. Its durable stages are `pending`, `drained`, `copied`, and `purged`: `pending` fences the old generation and invalidates screen state; after all leases drain, `drained` permits copying retained cache entries; `copied` records a complete manifest of copied exact keys and data/metadata digests before the old cache may be purged; `purged` permits activation of the new generation and journal removal. On restart after old-cache purge, verify the destination against that durable manifest without relying on the old cache. Generation activation is idempotent; any other session namespace means session replacement and must not be changed. Every stage is idempotent on process restart. Startup recovery owns retries and blocks private playback until completion.
- Remove or replace playlist rows only for media types whose selected-space snapshot is complete and revision-consistent. Preserve existing rows and their metadata for unavailable types, including videos when video enumeration is disabled. Preserve unrelated providers. The rebuilt playlist combines verified current rows with retained unavailable-type rows; it must not interpret a disabled feed as an empty library. Cached photos can play offline when their bytes remain available. Private videos currently require an online signed playback URL and are not promised offline playback.
- Persist the verified removal set, source origin and space, complete media types, revision and new operation generation in private durable storage in the same transaction that records `pending`. Persist stable scoped identities, not tokens or signed URLs. Recovery consumes this frozen set; it must not refetch or infer absence when offline. If recording any of it fails, leave the existing playlist and cache untouched and retry later.
- For the one-time legacy migration, copy and verify reachable retained entries into a separate cache before purging the old cache. Unknown older variants may need an online reload, as Al approved. For future writes, record each exact key against its scoped media identity durably before writing, and selectively evict all recorded variants on deletion. After the old generation is revoked and drained, issue a new operation generation within the same session namespace; old leases must remain invalid and cannot be revived. This generation fence is distinct from the cache namespace; existing confirmed-deletion and sign-out behavior remains separate. Cache cleanup must prevent stale in-flight writes from repopulating evicted keys.
- Hold the same session replacement and cleanup serialization across ownership validation and each mutation, including row removal, byte eviction, and journal finalization. Validate original namespace, origin and space before every destructive stage. If ownership changes, stop the old operation without touching the replacement session's shared cache or rows; handle any remaining journal through the serialized replacement or sign-out workflow. Test session replacement immediately before and during every stage.
- Recovery applies only the frozen removal set. New items are appended independently on the next successful feed refresh; recovery does not need their snapshot or a network connection. Track video capability alongside the revision in freshness checks so a capability change forces a new full feed even when the revision is unchanged.
- If live cleanup fails or times out, keep the journal and playback fence in place. A process-wide recovery worker retries with bounded exponential backoff while the process remains alive; startup recovery resumes after a restart. Only one owner may advance the journal at a time, and retries stop when sign-out supersedes it.
- Create `pending` while holding the session replacement and cleanup serialization, after rechecking the session namespace, origin, space and playlist identity against the snapshot. If any differ, discard the candidate and refetch; only then may the refresh lease be released and drainage begin. The persisted removal set and journal creation must commit atomically.
- In the same database transaction that deletes verified rows, clear pair and triple references and collage markers on retained selected-space rows that name removed items. Update `totalMediaItems` from the actual remaining row count, invalidate saved paging positions and rebuild all in-memory windows and mode indexes before resuming. Unrelated providers and retained unavailable types must remain present.
- Use all-or-nothing reconciliation for each full fetch: a failed photo or video page aborts the entire snapshot. An explicitly disabled video feed is unavailable rather than a failed page, so a complete versioned photo feed can authorize photo removal while video rows remain untouched. A later capability change forces a fresh full fetch.
- Serialize this journal with sign-out and confirmed deletion under the existing cleanup and sign-out locks. A sign-out supersedes reconciliation: it must complete the broader purge and cannot restore or rotate a signed-out session. Session replacement must never apply an old journal to the new namespace. Release the refresh lease before starting invalidation and drainage.
- A full sign-out or confirmed deletion must also purge any detached migration destination before deleting its journal or manifest. Its crash-recovery marker must retain enough information to find both source and destination until both are cleared; clearing only the currently active cache is insufficient. Do not wire migration until this is implemented and tested at every interruption point.
- Invalidate current portrait composition, cached pair and triple plans, displayed-slide history, mode indexes, preloads, pending navigation and in-flight media loads. Rebuild those from authorized remaining items; old positional history must not resolve to different items after row changes. Preserve user rotation choices separately from disposable planning history.
- Verify process termination at every stage, retries, simultaneous sign-out, session change, playback while deletion is detected, deleted members of two- and three-photo collages, stale preloads and history, unrelated providers, empty libraries, unavailable video, failed pages and changed revisions. Exercise an offline TV that reconnects after a phone deletion.

## Design questions for review

The existing deletion cleanup purges the entire playlist, including unrelated providers, and the screen invalidation listener stops playback. A new reconciliation path needs a recoverable, namespace-scoped cleanup that does not lose unrelated sources or permanently blank the screen. Establish exact restart ownership, journal transitions, cache key invalidation and interaction with concurrent sign-out before implementation. Confirm whether revision support is enabled on the intended server and whether its deletion triggers cover every removal path. Test failed pages, video capability changes, session switching, concurrent playback and process death at each journal transition.

Existing private image cache keys include the complete request URL, including crop dimensions and focus revision. The current code has no per-media inventory of historical variants. Selective eviction therefore cannot be assumed to work for existing caches. The approved one-time migration purges unknown legacy variants after copying reachable retained entries; subsequent writes require an inventory.

Inspection of the installed Coil 3.6.3 bytecode shows `RealDiskCache` hashes each key with SHA-256 before lookup and removal. Its network metadata contains response status, timestamps and headers, not the cache key. These observations do not prove every stored response omits a useful identifying header, but the current client cannot assume the original media identity can be recovered from disk entries. A bounded migration experiment must use synthetic media and establish whether safely preserving reachable retained variants across a full legacy-cache purge satisfies offline behavior before choosing that route.

The synthetic `KrillLegacyCacheMigrationTest` exercises copying a known key's data and metadata to a separate Coil cache before clearing the original. It also demonstrates the limitation: an unknown retained variant is not copied. Al accepts that limitation for the one-time migration. The migration must enumerate and copy reachable original and current display and panel variants for every retained image, verify successful copies, and purge the entire old cache only after the copies and journal are durable. If copying a reachable retained item fails, retain the old cache and retry; do not silently purge. Any unknown older retained variants may reload online. This test is not proof of process-death recovery, session fencing or production migration. Those remain prerequisites.

## Implementation status (10 November)

Implemented and unit tested, but not called by the running app:

- The journal, its stages and the copy manifest (Room schema 14). The journal row is the durable recovery marker. While it exists, `MediaService` refuses to build a playlist that includes the private library.
- `KrillReconciliationExecutor.runToPurged` drives `pending`, `drained`, `copied` and `purged`. `complete` also records the new generation, switches the active cache to the verified destination, deletes the journal and reopens the fence. Every step can be repeated after an interruption. A test interrupts the purge, the manifest check and the activation, then retries each.
- Sign-out and confirmed deletion purge a detached destination cache before they delete the journal.

Not implemented, so reconciliation stays off:

- Nothing creates a journal. The feed refresh does not yet compute removal candidates or call `begin`.
- Startup recovery and screen restart. `ScreenController` registers its playlist owner and records the cache epoch when it is constructed, but recovery needs the playlist gate to have no owners. A screen built before recovery would therefore block it, and after recovery it would skip saves and private video. Startup recovery must run, with bounded retries, before `DreamActivity` and `TestActivity` construct the screen. After a live cleanup, a stopped screen must be rebuilt. Both changes need proof on the TV.
- The one-time legacy cache migration.

Enable these only together, with on-device proof of process death at each stage.
